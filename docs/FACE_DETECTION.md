# Optional local face detection

Owner-approved YuNet detection was implemented 2026-10-07. On 2026-10-08, the owner requested optional recognition across future clips. OpenCV SFace now compares clear sampled faces against people explicitly named on the **Faces** page. It does not infer demographics, verify legal identity, or control the camera. Qwen remains responsible for separate activity descriptions.

## Using it

Open Activity, select a recording and choose **Detect faces** in recording details. When processing finishes, the panel shows the largest number of faces visible in any sampled frame, timestamps to review in the original video, and size/sharpness flags. Still images have one sample. The original recording stays available independently of detector success. A failed retry preserves the previous accepted result.

Faces → **Detection settings** has an optional automatic switch. It takes effect immediately, defaults off, and does not change the Qwen setting. After enabling it, only newly discovered, nonhistorical Blink cloud/Sync Module clips with a recording time within the past day are eligible. Old history and manual imports require an explicit request. Disabling pauses queued automatic requests; an automatic result finishing after the switch is disabled is discarded. Manual requests continue. No subscription or retrieval capability is implied by detection.

Counts describe faces in a frame, **not unique visitors**. The detector samples at most 48 frames, spread across a clip of up to five minutes; shorter clips use approximately four samples per second. It can still miss brief appearances between samples. “No faces detected” does not prove nobody was present. A person facing away may appear clearly to the vision model while leaving no face for YuNet or SFace to process. Camera angle, lighting, motion, compression and occlusion can affect results. For such a clip, the owner can put a known name in **Your description**; future automatic recognition still requires a clear face sample. Review the original recording.

## Naming people and later matches

First manually detect faces in an Activity recording, or opt into automatic detection for new Blink clips. Open **Faces** in the main navigation. **Other faces** shows up to three crops per tentative group from the newest saved reports; **Load older detections** pages through the rest of the scanned library. Select up to 12 images across cards, choose a new or existing person, and assign them in one action. Groups of similar unnamed faces are tentative and may contain different people, so check the original recordings before naming. **Recognize familiar people** below also offers individual sampled frames for manual selection, renaming and removal. The source frame is regenerated from the original recording through an authenticated, no-store route; its SHA-256 must match the saved detection report. Nothing is uploaded.

**Suggested matches** separately gathers individual unlabeled faces by possible saved person. The owner can use **Gather all saved scans** to walk all report pages, open each source clip, remove an image from the pending group, then approve the remaining shown images for that person in one action. Set-aside images stay unnamed and can be restored during the current review; suggestions are recomputed on a later visit, so setting one aside is not a permanent rejection. Approval labels only the reviewed face boxes in saved reports and makes the name visible in Activity. It does **not** add every image as a training sample or change the 32-sample limit; adding a clear training example remains a separate action. The save is atomic and rejects the whole selection if a source report, frame hash or face box changed during review. Similarity-based groups can be wrong, especially for small or blurred images, and never establish identity without owner review.

A saved-person card appears on the first Faces-page load for every saved profile, even if its detections are only in older recordings. Up to three representative labeled crops are selected from the entire saved scan library without loading the original media during gallery construction; the protected thumbnail route regenerates images when displayed. The card counts owner-confirmed face boxes across **all** saved scan reports, plus model-assigned matches separately. Its "grouped in loaded scans" count refers only to gallery pages currently loaded for review. A saved profile with no remaining source recording still appears without a crop. The library-wide counts update after an approval and remain available after refreshing the page. Counts are face detections, not unique appearances or a measure of recognition accuracy.

The bulk approval route has a dedicated 1 MiB request-body bound for groups containing hundreds of reviewed face references. Other ordinary API requests retain their 16 KiB bound. The approval contract accepts at most 2,000 unique faces in one atomic save.

Each saved person has normalized 128-value SFace exemplars encrypted in a Windows DPAPI envelope under the local data folder. The owner can add up to 32 labelled samples. New samples remain separate to preserve different useful poses; existing legacy profiles retain their former average as an exemplar when another sample is added. Raw crops and per-clip embeddings are not persisted or returned by the API. A detection job compares suitable faces against each person's exemplars inside the bounded CPU worker. A best match must pass cosine score 0.45 and exceed the next candidate by 0.08; small/blurred faces and uncertain comparisons remain unnamed. These are conservative engineering thresholds, **not measured accuracy**. The recording details display “Possible match” and link to the original timeline for review. The clip used to name a face gains that label immediately. Other already processed clips need **Detect faces again** or **Rescan recent 20 videos** to use a newly added person; future queued clips use current profiles. Renaming changes the displayed name, while removing a person stops future matching and leaves old references unresolved.

Activity cards and recording details can use a saved name in place of the generic person subject: “Alex moves from left to right. A black car is visible.” This applies only when the latest analysis describes exactly one person, the face report contains one matched profile and no sampled frame contains multiple faces, and the generated description has exactly one generic person subject. Unknown, deleted-profile, missing-analysis and ambiguous/multiple-person cases stay generic. Owner descriptions and structured owner labels take precedence and are never substituted. A visible **possible face match** note retains the source/uncertainty; recording details expose the **Original AI description**. Qwen's stored observation and summary stay untouched: the name is resolved from the protected face result at read time, so a later rename or removal updates Activity without rerunning Qwen. Read-time presentation does not add names to notification payloads, search indices or Qwen inputs.

Recognition starts only after the owner saves a person. Detection and raw recording preservation work without SFace. Automatic face detection remains a separate default-off switch. No face-based notifications, auto-disarming, or emergency actions are added.

**Automatically add exceptionally strong face matches as samples** is a separate Faces-page switch. With it enabled, future face scans can add at most one SFace exemplar per person per recording. A profile must already have eight owner-assigned samples. The scan must have two clear frames with the same saved profile, detector score at least 0.90 and SFace cosine at least 0.75. The selected sample is checked again against **owner-assigned** exemplars: cosine at least 0.75 and at least 0.15 above the next profile. Near-duplicate features, clips already used for an automatic sample, profiles at the 32-sample cap, and clips manually labelled for that profile are skipped. Automatic exemplars never become the seed evidence for another automatic addition. Counts of owner-assigned and automatically added samples appear in the saved-person list. These numerical cutoffs are conservative heuristics on this camera, **not calibrated certainty or a guarantee of identity**. Review possible names in Activity and remove a profile if it is wrong.

Images are resized to a maximum 960-pixel edge. The size flag uses a face rectangle smaller than 48 pixels on its shortest edge. The low-sharpness flag uses grayscale Laplacian variance below 80. These are uncalibrated engineering heuristics, not measured accuracy or a suitability assessment. The v3 detector score threshold is 0.80 (older reports used 0.90); this is a model output threshold, not an “80% accurate” claim. Lower-scored detections need extra owner review.

## Explicit setup

The core bootstrap does not install detector packages or model weights. With a bootstrapped Windows Python environment:

```powershell
./scripts/enable-face-detection.ps1
# After reviewing and approving the displayed installation:
./scripts/enable-face-detection.ps1 -Install
```

The installer adds only `opencv-python-headless==4.14.0.94` and `numpy==2.5.3` to Porchlight’s `.venv`, constrained against the base lock. It downloads only the pinned YuNet detector and its license to `C:\local-models\yunet-face-detection`. The file size and SHA-256 must match [the model manifest](../services/porchlight/face_model.json). A differing existing file is preserved. No global Ollama configuration is changed, and automatic detection is not enabled by installation.

For recognition, preview `./scripts/enable-face-recognition.ps1`, then explicitly run `./scripts/enable-face-recognition.ps1 -Install`. It installs the pinned 38,696,353-byte SFace model and Apache-2.0 license to `C:\local-models\sface-recognition`; it uses the same OpenCV wheel as YuNet. [The SFace manifest](../services/porchlight/sface_model.json) pins the URL, byte count, and SHA-256. Restart Porchlight afterward. The first model load is CPU-only; Ollama and global GPU settings are unchanged. A missing or mismatched SFace model disables enrollment/matching but leaves YuNet detection available.

Another machine can use `-ModelDirectory 'D:\models\yunet'` and set `PORCHLIGHT_FACE_MODEL` to its full `.onnx` path before starting Porchlight. Restart Porchlight after installation. Missing packages, changed package versions, a missing/mismatched model or unavailable FFmpeg make the optional feature unavailable while the core app continues working. Tested environment: Windows x64, Python 3.12.10, OpenCV 4.14.0.94, NumPy 2.5.3. Other interpreter/platform combinations require their own check.

## Data and execution

A separate persistent `face_detect` queue caps active work at 30 jobs. One CPU inference child runs at a time with two OpenCV threads. The parent extracts bounded local JPEG samples with FFmpeg and passes them through stdin; the detector child has no application tool or network interface. It runs for at most 30 seconds; total per-job work is bounded at 220 seconds. Cancellation kills and reaps the child. Failures retry at most three attempts with bounded backoff. Restart recovery requeues interrupted requests. Jobs freeze the detector policy and model digest; changed contracts require a new request. Older reports remain readable; **Detect faces again** uses the denser v3 policy and 0.80 detector threshold.

Only frame hashes, offsets, dimensions, rectangles, detector scores, quality flags, and optional matched profile IDs/scores are stored in SQLite. Reusable templates and automatic sample source IDs per person are stored in the local Windows DPAPI envelope. Face crops and per-clip gallery embeddings are not stored. The gallery pages through all accepted reports in recording-date order, considering 20 recordings and up to 300 candidates per page, spread across recordings; it regenerates authenticated, no-store crops on demand. Its groupings and possible-name suggestions are review aids, not identity claims. Authentication protects results, names, candidates and regenerated sample frames; CSRF protects enrollment, rename, deletion, rescans and automatic-setting changes. Active jobs protect their source recording from deletion/retention. Deleting a recording removes its report and detector jobs. Backups retain reports as private local data, omit external model weights, and restore automatic detection paused. DPAPI templates are tied to the current Windows account and should be re-enrolled after transfer to another machine/account.

## Source and license intake

YuNet 2023mar is obtained from [OpenCV Zoo](https://github.com/opencv/opencv_zoo/tree/26cc381e4d2594bb9f47a26eb8fd96c94a13660d/models/face_detection_yunet), pinned commit `26cc381e4d2594bb9f47a26eb8fd96c94a13660d`. The 232,589-byte model digest is `8f2383e4dd3cfbb4553ea8718107fc0423210dc964f9f4280604804ed2552fa4`. The directory’s [MIT license](https://github.com/opencv/opencv_zoo/blob/26cc381e4d2594bb9f47a26eb8fd96c94a13660d/models/face_detection_yunet/LICENSE) is retained alongside the downloaded model and in `notices/YUNET-LICENSE.txt`. Integration uses the [OpenCV detector and recognizer API](https://docs.opencv.org/4.x/d0/dd4/tutorial_dnn_face.html); no upstream application is copied.

The SFace 2021dec model is from the same pinned OpenCV Zoo commit, under the directory [Apache-2.0 license](https://github.com/opencv/opencv_zoo/blob/26cc381e4d2594bb9f47a26eb8fd96c94a13660d/models/face_recognition_sface/LICENSE). Its SHA-256 is `0ba9fbfa01b5270c96627c4ef784da859931e02f04419c829e83484087c34e79`; exact size is 38,696,353 bytes. The license is retained with the downloaded model and in `notices/SFACE-LICENSE.txt`. Training-data/commercial-use provenance has [an unresolved upstream clarification request](https://github.com/opencv/opencv_zoo/issues/313); review that separately before distributing a commercial product.

OpenCV’s package declares Apache-2.0; NumPy declares BSD-3-Clause AND 0BSD AND MIT AND Zlib AND CC0-1.0. Installed wheel notices, including third-party notices, are retained in `notices/python/`. Check them before distribution; package declarations alone are not release clearance. [The advisory intake](FACE_DEPENDENCY_AUDIT.json) checked only the two public package names/versions against OSV and found zero matching advisories on the recorded date. This does not certify security or model accuracy. Weights remain outside the repository and normal backup.

## Validation

Native detection and the API/UI journey are tested using generated solid-color media. SFace loading, finite 128-value output, synthetic-row match wiring, profile lifecycle and authenticated/CSRF routes are tested. Positive-result rendering, failure/recovery and queue behavior use clearly synthetic detections; these are not face-accuracy measurements. A one-clip real-camera probe processes imagery locally and prints aggregate metrics only:

```powershell
./stop.ps1
./.venv/Scripts/python.exe scripts/probe-latest-faces.py
./dev.ps1
```

On 2026-10-07, the latest connected-camera clip completed in 1.86 seconds with 10 samples and zero detected faces. No private imagery entered coding tools. This measures execution on one clip, not face recall, identity accuracy or general camera compatibility. Enrollment and future-clip recognition have not yet been measured on owner-labelled real footage. Owner visual review on daylight, night, small/blurred and partly visible faces remains pending. Current milestone work remains M3/M4 validation; the existing no-plan hardware and release gates remain open.
